Popout Authenticator — 2FA & OTP Codes

2FA authenticator for your browser. Encrypted codes in a popup, window or side panel, filled into the sites you allow.

Open source (MIT) · No account limits · No sign-up · No analytics

Your codes, where you need them

  • Open your codes in the toolbar popup, a separate window, or the side panel (sidebar in Firefox).
  • Search, groups, pinned accounts, and a compact view keep long lists tidy.
  • See which account belongs to the site you are on, and fill its code with one click, a right-click, or a keyboard shortcut.
  • Optional buttons next to code fields on the sites you choose.

Add accounts your way

  • Scan a QR code from the current tab, your camera, an image, or the clipboard.
  • Paste otpauth:// links, or type the secret by hand.
  • Import plain-text backups from many authenticator apps, migration QR codes, and standard credential exchange (CXF) files.
  • Supports TOTP, HOTP, Steam, and Battle.net codes with SHA-1, SHA-256, or SHA-512 and 6 to 10 digits.

Private and protected

  • Accounts are stored in an encrypted vault on your device (AES-256-GCM).
  • Lock it with a password (Argon2id), a recovery code, and optional passkey unlock (not available in Firefox). Choose when it locks automatically.
  • Optionally require device verification before a code is used, and hide codes with a keyboard shortcut you assign.
  • No analytics, no ads, no developer server. The source code is public.

Backup and recovery

  • Encrypted backups, plus exports to otpauth text, CXF, and QR codes.
  • Encrypted sync between your devices through your browser's own sync (Chrome, Edge, Firefox).
  • Recovery snapshots, a 30-day trash, and an account change history.
  • Clock check warns you when your computer's time would make codes fail.

Share a code, not the secret

Send a link that shows the codes for the next 5 to 60 minutes, with an optional password. The link never contains the secret.

Privacy

There is no developer server, no analytics, and no ads. A clock check asks time.akamai.com, timeapi.io, and cloudflare.com for the current time, with no account data; you can turn it off. On Chrome, Edge, and Firefox, browser sync carries your accounts, encrypted with your vault key, through the browser's own sync service once you set a password lock; you can turn it off. Details are in the privacy policy.

Privacy policy · Source code

Limits

  • There is no cloud backup. You have file backups and your browser's own sync.
  • Passkey unlock is not available in Firefox.
  • The Safari version is for macOS; there is no iOS version.
